# Building AI course project
Final project for building AI: MathMIND
MathMind Project.
MathMind is an AI-assisted learning tool designed to help young people with dyscalculia improve their number sense, basic arithmetic skills, and confidence in mathematics through adaptive exercises, personalized feedback, and pattern-based learning support.

BACKGROUND
Dyscalculia is a learning difficulty that affects a person’s ability to understand numbers, perform calculations, and grasp mathematical concepts. It is estimated to affect around 5% of the population, but many cases remain undiagnosed or unsupported throughout someones life. Some may assume that they "just are not good with numbers or math". But it is not about being good or bad. It is about supporting those students that have dyscalculia diagnoses. It is about moving beyond traditional math teaching methods which often move too quickly or rely too heavily on memorization, which can lead to frustration, anxiety, and avoidance of math-related tasks.

My motivation for this project is that many learners with dyscalculia are capable of understanding math concepts when they are presented in a different way like more visual, structured, and step-by-step methods. AI can help personalize learning so that students receive the exact level of support they need and following how the student may answer and reason math problems to see where the difficulty may lie and present certain lessons that would improve and exercise those challenge in a tailored way for that student. 

This topic is important because numeracy is essential in everyday life, from managing money to understanding time and measurements, and lack of support can have long-term educational, psychological, and social consequences.

DATA AND AI TECHNIQUES
Data sources.
The system would use student response data (correct/incorrect answers) including time taken per question, type of errors (e.g., number reversal, counting mistakes), difficulty levels of the exercises posed, and progress tracking over time.
In a prototype version, individual or classroom-style datasets can be used.

AI TECHNIQUES
The project would use a combination of adaptive learning algorithms (adjust difficulty based on performance), classification models (identify types of mistakes), recommendation systems (suggest next exercises) and simple neural networks or decision trees for pattern recognition. A basic version could start with rule-based adaptation (if wrong → easier task, if correct → slightly harder task), while more advanced versions could learn individual learning patterns.

WHERE AND WHO WOULD USE IT
MathMind would be used in schools (special education support), home learning environments (home schooled children or for homework), tutoring programs, and with learning disability support services outside or within educational settings. 

It is designed for children and teenagers with dyscalculia but it could also be extended to adults. It could also be used to help teachers supporting mixed-ability classrooms and to help parents helping children practice math at home. The system provides personalized exercises and feedback, helping learners build confidence without feeling overwhelmed.

PROJECT CHALLENGES

This project does not solve formal diagnosis of dyscalculia. This means it is not a diagnostic tool and neither does it try to replace human teachers, special needs teachers, developmental psychologists and others that assist children and youth in education settings. It also depends heavily on accurate labeling of student performance data and avoiding bias in adaptation to the diversity of youth. 

MATHMIND AND THE FUTURE
The project could be expanded by adding visual and interactive learning tools (number lines, blocks, animations)
integrating speech-based explanations for accessibility
-using real classroom datasets to improve personalization
-building a mobile app for continuous practice
-adding collaboration tools for teachers and parents
-expanding into other learning difficulties (e.g., dyslexia support for math word problems)
